# Mathematical specification for the 3-D TDGL–Maxwell solver

## 1. Modelling decision and validity range

The production baseline is a **magnetoquasistatic (MQS)**, gauge-invariant TDGL model. It retains superconducting and Ohmic normal currents and solves the magnetic field in the superconductor, conductors, insulators, and a surrounding vacuum truncation. It neglects displacement current and therefore does not propagate electromagnetic waves.

This is distinct from the **full electromagnetic-wave** model, which adds

$$
\partial_t\mathbf D=\epsilon\partial_t\mathbf E
=-\epsilon(\partial_{tt}\mathbf A+\nabla\partial_t\phi)
$$

to Ampère's law. Full-wave coupling is appropriate when radiation, a resonant cavity, or a device size comparable to the electromagnetic wavelength matters. It changes the PDE type, exterior radiation condition, initialization, and time resolution. It will be an optional later formulation, not silently mixed into the MQS solver.

TDGL itself is a phenomenological near-$T_c$ theory. Material coefficients must be calibrated for the intended dirty/gapless regime. Predictions at low temperature, for ultrafast nonequilibrium dynamics, or for quantitative S–N proximity may require Usadel, Eilenberger, or Bogoliubov–de Gennes models.

## 2. Domains and material regions

Let the bounded computational domain be

$$
\Omega=\Omega_s\cup\Omega_n\cup\Omega_i\cup\Omega_v,
$$

with conforming material interfaces:

- $\Omega_s$: superconducting material in which the complex order parameter is active;
- $\Omega_n$: normal metal, possibly coupled to $\Omega_s$ by a GL proximity model;
- $\Omega_i$: solid insulating material or void/cavity;
- $\Omega_v$: surrounding vacuum extending from the specimen to an artificial outer boundary $\Gamma_\infty=\partial\Omega$.

Let $\Omega_c=\Omega_s\cup\Omega_n$ denote electrically conducting material and $\Omega_{GL}$ denote the region in which $\psi$ is solved. The baseline choices are:

- no proximity: $\Omega_{GL}=\Omega_s$, with $\psi=0$ by absence—not a nodal constraint—in $\Omega_n\cup\Omega_i\cup\Omega_v$;
- phenomenological proximity: $\Omega_{GL}=\Omega_s\cup\Omega_n$, with material-dependent GL coefficients in the normal metal.

The electromagnetic unknowns are solved throughout $\Omega$. The vacuum region is therefore part of the model, not a display padding. Applied magnetic data are placed on $\Gamma_\infty$, sufficiently far from the specimen, or generated by explicit source currents. They are not imposed directly on a finite superconductor boundary, because that would overwrite the self-consistent screening and demagnetizing field.

## 3. Dimensional formulation

### 3.1 Potentials and fields

Use SI units, Cooper-pair charge $q=2e$ in magnitude, and the convention

$$
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A,
\qquad
\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf A-\nabla\phi,
\qquad
\mathbf D_{\!A}=\nabla-i\frac{q}{\hbar}\mathbf A.
$$

A gauge transformation by a sufficiently regular scalar $\chi$ is

$$
\mathbf A' = \mathbf A+\nabla\chi,
\quad
\phi'=\phi-\partial_t\chi,
\quad
\Psi'=\Psi\exp\!\left(i\frac{q}{\hbar}\chi\right).
$$

All physical observables and the numerical verification tests must be invariant under this transformation.

### 3.2 Free energy

For a GL-active region, the dimensional free energy is

$$
\mathcal G =
\int_{\Omega_{GL}}
\left[
\alpha(\mathbf x)|\Psi|^2
+\frac{\beta(\mathbf x)}{2}|\Psi|^4
+\frac{1}{2m^*(\mathbf x)}
\left|(-i\hbar\nabla-q\mathbf A)\Psi\right|^2
\right]dV
+\frac12\int_{\Omega}
(\mathbf B-\mathbf B_a)\cdot\mu^{-1}(\mathbf B-\mathbf B_a)\,dV.
$$

The last expression is a Gibbs-type finite-domain form when an applied field is prescribed at the remote boundary. Source-coil formulations require the corresponding source-work term instead. Energy accounting will be defined consistently with whichever outer closure is used.

### 3.3 Order-parameter evolution

The dissipative TDGL equation is

$$
\Gamma(\mathbf x)
\left(\partial_t+i\frac{q}{\hbar}\phi\right)\Psi
=
-\frac{\delta\mathcal G}{\delta\Psi^*}
=
-\alpha\Psi-\beta|\Psi|^2\Psi
+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m^*}\mathbf D_{\!A}^{,2}\Psi.
$$

More elaborate Kramer–Watts–Tobin relaxation can be added as a separately verified model; it is not assumed in the baseline.

### 3.4 Currents and MQS Maxwell equation

The superconducting current is

$$
\mathbf J_s
=\frac{q\hbar}{m^*}\operatorname{Im}(\Psi^*\nabla\Psi)
-\frac{q^2}{m^*}|\Psi|^2\mathbf A.
$$

The Ohmic normal current is

$$
\mathbf J_n=\boldsymbol\sigma(\mathbf x)\mathbf E
=-\boldsymbol\sigma(\partial_t\mathbf A+\nabla\phi).
$$

The MQS equations are

$$
\nabla\times\left(\mu^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf A\right)
=\mathbf J_s+\mathbf J_n+\mathbf J_{\rm ext},
\qquad
\nabla\cdot(\mathbf J_s+\mathbf J_n+\mathbf J_{\rm ext})=0.
$$

In vacuum and ideal insulator $\boldsymbol\sigma=0$ and $\mathbf J_s=0$, so the vector potential still solves the source-free Maxwell equation. In a normal metal $\Psi=0$ unless the proximity option is enabled, but $\boldsymbol\sigma>0$, so eddy and transport currents remain.

The charge-neutral/current-continuity equation determines $\phi$ in conducting regions. If capacitive charge accumulation is required, it must be introduced explicitly rather than hidden in this constraint.

## 4. Dimensionless formulation

Choose reference superconducting coefficients $\alpha_s<0$, $\beta_s>0$, and $m_s^*$, and define

$$
\Psi_0=\sqrt{\frac{|\alpha_s|}{\beta_s}},\quad
\xi=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_s^*|\alpha_s|}},\quad
\lambda=\sqrt{\frac{m_s^*}{\mu_0q^2\Psi_0^2}},\quad
\kappa=\frac{\lambda}{\xi}.
$$

Use

$$
\mathbf x=\xi\hat{\mathbf x},\quad
t=t_0\hat t,\quad
\Psi=\Psi_0\psi,\quad
\mathbf A=\frac{\hbar}{q\xi}\mathbf A',\quad
\mathbf B=\frac{\hbar}{q\xi^2}\mathbf B',\quad
\phi=\frac{\hbar}{qt_0}\varphi,
$$

where $t_0=\Gamma_s/|\alpha_s|$, and $B_0=\hbar/(q\xi^2)=\Phi_0/(2\pi\xi^2)$. Dropping primes, a flexible dimensionless system is

$$
u(\mathbf x)(\partial_t+i\varphi)\psi
=(\nabla-i\mathbf A)\cdot
K(\mathbf x)(\nabla-i\mathbf A)\psi
+a(\mathbf x)\psi-b(\mathbf x)|\psi|^2\psi,
\quad \mathbf x\in\Omega_{GL},
$$

$$
s(\mathbf x)(\partial_t\mathbf A+\nabla\varphi)
+\kappa^2\nabla\times\left(\mu_r^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf A\right)
-\mathbf j_s(\psi,\mathbf A)
=\mathbf j_{\rm ext},
\quad \mathbf x\in\Omega,
$$

with

$$
\mathbf j_s
=K(\mathbf x)\operatorname{Im}
\left[\psi^*(\nabla-i\mathbf A)\psi\right]
\quad\text{in }\Omega_{GL},
$$

and zero extension elsewhere. Here $s$ is a nondimensional conductivity, $K$ is an effective inverse-mass tensor/scalar, and $a,b,u,\mu_r$ may vary by material. The sign convention is such that homogeneous superconducting material has $a=b=1$, giving equilibrium $|\psi|=1$. All input/output routines will retain an explicit scale object so dimensionless results can be converted back to SI without ad hoc factors.

Taking the divergence of the electromagnetic equation yields the weak current-continuity equation for $\varphi$. It must be solved rather than inferred after the fact when transport current or spatially varying conductivity is present.

## 5. Unknowns and function spaces

On a tetrahedral mesh with material-conforming subdomains, the intended continuous spaces are

$$
\psi\in H^1(\Omega_{GL};\mathbb C),
\qquad
\mathbf A\in H(\mathrm{curl};\Omega),
\qquad
\varphi\in H^1(\Omega_c)/\mathbb R.
$$

The Coulomb-gauge multiplier $p$ lies in an $H^1$-type scalar space with its constant nullspace removed. Optional mixed variables use

$$
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A\in H(\mathrm{div};\Omega),
\qquad
\mathbf J\in H(\mathrm{div};\Omega_c).
$$

Discrete spaces will respect the de Rham sequence:

- complex Lagrange P1 initially, then P2, for $\psi$;
- first-family Nédélec edge elements, order 1 initially, for $\mathbf A$;
- Lagrange elements for $\varphi$ and gauge multiplier $p$;
- Raviart–Thomas or BDM face elements if $\mathbf B$ or $\mathbf J$ is made primary.

The edge orientation is global and deterministic. Incidence matrices for discrete gradient, curl, and divergence will be built explicitly and tested for $CG=0$ and $DC=0$. This is essential in nonconvex and multiply connected geometries.

## 6. Gauge condition and topological constraints

### 6.1 Production gauge

The recommended production formulation uses Coulomb gauge,

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0,
$$

enforced weakly with a scalar Lagrange multiplier (or an equivalent augmented formulation). A compatible normal trace condition and zero-mean constraint remove the ordinary scalar gauge nullspace. The exact boundary condition depends on the outer-field splitting:

- total potential formulation: impose a compatible tangential/outer datum for $\mathbf A$ and fix the remaining gauge;
- scattered potential $\mathbf A=\mathbf A_a+\mathbf a$: impose a homogeneous condition on $\mathbf a$ at $\Gamma_\infty$, with $\mathbf A_a$ analytically or numerically gauge-consistent.

The physical electric scalar potential $\varphi$ is retained. This supports voltage, conductivity contrast, current injection, and current conservation. A temporal-gauge branch $\varphi=0$ will be implemented only for validation against published magnetic-only tests.

### 6.2 Multiply connected domains

For rings, holes, handles, or disconnected conductors, gradient gauge modes are not the only null modes. The discrete $H(\mathrm{curl})$ space contains harmonic representatives associated with the first cohomology group. The mesh module must therefore compute a cycle/cohomology basis or cuts and impose physically selected circulation/fluxoid constraints.

For every independent superconducting loop $C_k$, the fluxoid condition is

$$
\oint_{C_k}\left(\mathbf A+
\frac{m^*}{q^2|\Psi|^2}\mathbf J_s\right)\cdot d\mathbf l
=n_k\Phi_0,
\qquad n_k\in\mathbb Z,
$$

away from phase-slip events. Initial winding sectors $n_k$ are input data or inferred from the initial phase. A phase slip can change a sector only when $|\psi|$ vanishes across an appropriate path. Artificially pinning harmonic coefficients would destroy this physics.

## 7. Boundary and interface conditions

### 7.1 Superconductor–vacuum or superconductor–insulator

For a non-pair-breaking boundary,

$$
\mathbf n\cdot K(\nabla-i\mathbf A)\psi=0.
$$

Finite pair breaking uses a de Gennes Robin condition,

$$
\mathbf n\cdot K(\nabla-i\mathbf A)\psi=-\gamma_b\psi,
$$

where $\gamma_b=0$ is the insulating limit and the extrapolation length must be supplied from material/interface data. This order-parameter condition does **not** prescribe the magnetic field. Maxwell transmission into $\Omega_v$ is solved simultaneously.

### 7.2 Superconductor–normal-metal interface

Two explicitly different models are supported.

1. **No proximity:** solve $\psi$ only in $\Omega_s$; use a de Gennes boundary condition on the S side. The N side conducts normal current and participates in Maxwell.
2. **GL proximity:** solve $\psi$ on both sides with $a_n<0$ or otherwise calibrated normal-metal coefficients. Enforce continuity of $\psi$ for an ideal transparent interface and continuity of the weighted covariant flux
   $$
   \llbracket\mathbf n\cdot K(\nabla-i\mathbf A)\psi\rrbracket=0.
   $$
   A finite interface resistance/barrier is represented by matched Robin/Kupriyanov–Lukichev-type conditions after deriving a consistent variational interface energy.

The second option is phenomenological near $T_c$. It must be labeled as such in benchmark results.

### 7.3 Electromagnetic interfaces

With no imposed sheet current or magnetic monopole,

$$
\llbracket\mathbf n\times\mathbf E\rrbracket=0,
\qquad
\llbracket\mathbf n\times\mathbf H\rrbracket=0,
\qquad
\llbracket\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf B\rrbracket=0,
\qquad
\llbracket\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf J\rrbracket=0
$$

where applicable. Known surface current $\mathbf K_s$ changes the second relation to $\mathbf n\times(\mathbf H_2-\mathbf H_1)=\mathbf K_s$. Curl-conforming elements impose tangential compatibility naturally; coefficient-weighted flux conditions enter the weak form.

### 7.4 Remote outer boundary and unbounded exterior

The baseline truncates vacuum at $\Gamma_\infty$, several sample diameters away, and uses one of:

- total-field Dirichlet/tangential potential data consistent with a uniform applied $\mathbf B_a$;
- a scattered-potential condition $\mathbf a\to0$ for $\mathbf A=\mathbf A_a+\mathbf a$;
- a magnetic scalar-potential or infinite-element closure in source-free exterior subregions.

Every result must include a vacuum-padding convergence study. The preferred later production closure is a Maxwell FEM–BEM coupling on the specimen/exterior interface, which represents the unbounded MQS domain without a huge vacuum mesh. A radiating DtN/PML boundary belongs to the separate full-wave model.

## 8. Applied fields and transport currents

### Applied magnetic field

A uniform field may be represented by a gauge-consistent background potential, for example $\mathbf A_a=\tfrac12\mathbf B_a\times\mathbf r$, with the scattered field solved in all regions. Time-dependent fields require the induced electric field to be consistent with $-\partial_t\mathbf A_a-\nabla\varphi_a$. Nonuniform fields are generated by prescribed source coils $\mathbf J_{\rm ext}$ outside the superconductor or by imported source-field maps satisfying Maxwell compatibility.

### Transport current

On terminal surfaces $\Gamma_I^\pm$, prescribe either total current

$$
\int_{\Gamma_I^+}\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=I(t),
\qquad
\int_{\Gamma_I^-}\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=-I(t),
$$

or terminal potential, but not both. Distributed Neumann data should be used only when physically known; otherwise a terminal multiplier enforces the integral constraint while the current profile emerges from the coupled solve. Insulating surfaces have $\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf n=0$. A single reference potential fixes the additive constant.

## 9. Initial conditions

Inputs are $\psi_0$ on $\Omega_{GL}$, $\mathbf A_0$ on $\Omega$, and, when the full-wave model is selected, $\partial_t\mathbf A_0$. They must satisfy boundary data, the selected gauge, and Maxwell/current constraints to numerical tolerance.

Supported initialization paths will include:

- Meissner state: $\psi_0\approx1$ with a magnetostatic solve for $\mathbf A_0$;
- seeded vortices: phase winding and amplitude depletion around prescribed curves;
- continuation: the converged state from a nearby field, current, or temperature;
- random/noisy nucleation: reproducible random seed and documented noise covariance.

An initial projection solves the discrete gauge/constraint system. In multiply connected domains the desired winding/fluxoid sector is stated explicitly.

## 10. Observables and diagnostics

The postprocessor will compute, with SI and dimensionless labels:

- $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf A-\nabla\phi$;
- $\mathbf J_s$, $\mathbf J_n$, imposed source current, and total current;
- GL condensation, kinetic, magnetic, and total Gibbs/free-energy components;
- magnetization from $\tfrac{1}{2V_s}\int\mathbf r\times\mathbf J\,dV$, cross-checked against volume/outer-field identities;
- terminal voltage as the electrochemical potential difference or the gauge-invariant line integral $-\int\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l$, with path-independence checked in steady conditions;
- vortex cores as low-$|\psi|$ tubes, vortex lines by phase-winding/plaquette flux, line orientation, length, and endpoints;
- magnetic flux and fluxoid around a homology basis;
- current-continuity residual, gauge residual, interface jumps, nonlinear residual, and energy/work balance.

Vortex identification will not rely on a single amplitude threshold: phase winding and magnetic flux provide independent confirmation.

## 11. Recommended first production formulation

The first complete formulation should be:

- fitted tetrahedral mesh of $\Omega_s\cup\Omega_n\cup\Omega_i\cup\Omega_v$;
- complex P1 $\psi$, first-order Nédélec $\mathbf A$, P1 $\varphi$, and a P1 gauge multiplier;
- MQS coupling with spatially varying $\sigma,\mu,K,a,b,u$;
- weak Coulomb gauge plus explicit scalar and harmonic nullspace constraints;
- backward Euler with damped Newton and a Picard fallback;
- scattered-field vacuum truncation, followed by a vacuum-size error study;
- no-proximity S–N model first, then the coefficient-based GL proximity model.

This choice maximizes auditability. Higher order, second-order time stepping, adaptive remeshing, and FEM–BEM coupling are promoted only after the verification gates in [`verification-plan.md`](verification-plan.md) pass.
